# City Council Approves Plan to Replace Streetlights with Adaptive Lighting

*By Staff Reporter, Harbor Point Ledger*

The Harbor Point City Council approved a plan on Tuesday to replace the city's 4,200 streetlights with adaptive LED fixtures that dim when streets are empty and brighten when sensors detect people or cars.

The project will cost approximately $6.8 million over three years. Officials said the new lights will use about 60 percent less electricity, and that the savings will cover most of the cost within a decade.

The vote was 5 to 2. Council member Dana Ruiz said the change will improve safety and lower utility bills. Council member Tom Becker voted against the plan, saying residents had not been given enough time to comment.

Some residents raised questions about the sensors. The city said the fixtures detect motion and light levels only, do not record video, and do not store data about individual people.

Local business owners on Front Street said they supported brighter lighting in the evening, when the shopping district is busiest. Several taxpayers asked whether the money could be spent on road repairs instead.

Installation will begin in the spring, starting with the waterfront and the downtown district. The city will hold two public meetings next month to answer questions about the project.
