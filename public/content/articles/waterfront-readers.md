# City Tests License-Plate Readers at the Waterfront

*By Jordan Hale, Ball Street Journal*

Harbor Point began a 90-day test of license-plate readers along the waterfront on Wednesday, mounting six units on existing light poles from the ferry terminal to the municipal pier.

The devices photograph plates, not faces, and the city says each record is deleted after seven days unless it is tied to an open case. Police Chief Lena Ortiz said the test is meant to recover stolen cars and to find people listed as missing, not to write tickets for parking.

The contract with the vendor is $84,000 for the trial. If the council votes to keep the system, the annual cost would be about $310,000.

Shop owners on Front Street said they wanted help with a rise in catalytic-converter thefts. A neighborhood group circulated a letter asking the city to publish weekly totals of how often the readers are searched, and by whom.

The public-safety committee will hear the first report in January. The cameras can be taken down at the end of the trial if the council does not extend them.
